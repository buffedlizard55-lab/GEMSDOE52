Was co-training independent enough to test?

On 2,281 spatial blocks containing 4,719,699 labelled-negative proxy predictions, the largest absolute view-error association was Spearman 0.1052 (false-positive rate at 0.5); the negative-MSE Spearman coefficient was 0.0909. The registered stop threshold was 0.6, so the mechanism was allowed to run. This low proxy correlation does not demonstrate conditional independence of the underlying view errors or guarantee safe pseudo-labels. The direct outcome is more decisive for this experiment: after permitted exchange, co-training lost in every holdout fold.

Per fold, pseudo-candidate components were formed from finite confident/abstaining-view scores inside the training-domain mask only. The selector kept selected pixels in that domain, rejected observed components containing forbidden catalogue-collar pixels or spanning more than one 50×50-pixel block, and never split an observed component to satisfy the cap. Because scores outside the training mask were not generated, it cannot tell whether a candidate component continues across the masked training/evaluation boundary; “whole” is verified only within the observed train-domain candidate field. This does not show evaluation-label leakage, but limits the claim of complete-component handling. There was a matched single-view arm and max-union control. See the machine-readable H57 holdout receipt and execution provenance review.

A-only geology is a hypothesis, not a declaration

The A-only diagnostic includes 1,137 connected components representing 36,540 candidate cells; the emitted map selected 3,030 cells from those components. The linked CSV gives every component’s coordinates, size, A/B scores, distance to the supplied catalogue, elongation/axis, relevant band values and global z-scores, local gradient channels, an explicitly tentative covered-fault interpretation, alternative lithologic/intrusive/processing explanations, and review status.

The intended mechanism is physically plausible but non-unique: a buried fault may juxtapose or offset density/magnetic bodies and alter cover-scale electrical/thermal conditions while lacking a sharp topographic expression. The same geophysical edge can instead be an intrusion, lithologic contact, regional trend, inversion boundary, or survey artifact. The A-only map is not proof, and the audit does not claim that every component is a fault.

B-only pixels remain a separate diagnostic: 28,322 candidate cells across 2,132 components; none were selected in this emission. They are possible surface artifacts (for example, drainage/road/fan edges), not automatically discarded truth.

The published metric uses a 300 m triangular distance kernel, α=0.2 on weighted false positives and β=0.8 on weighted false negatives. Thus a plausible explanation for a strong sparse submission is ranking cells near hidden faults while avoiding unsupported mass; deleting low-credit/false-positive cells could help. But the hidden test truth and upload-level score receipt are unavailable here, so the causal reason for H33’s reported score cannot be established from its raster alone. Ranked hypotheses and next experiments

The five candidates were written in the frozen hypothesis record before the H57 feature construction and holdout. They are qualitative expected DTI opportunity per cost, not forecasts:

  1. Corrected-view asymmetric buried-structure disagreement (H57-H1). Tested; holdout failed. Do not spend a slot on this configuration.
  2. Conductivity termination at a potential-field intersection. Band 17 with magnetic/gravity bands and basement depth; seek persistent cross-field junction geometry, not conductivity highs alone. Medium cost; boundaries may reflect inversion or lithology.
  3. Radiometric flank contrast aligned with a structural step. Band 6 plus optional independently sourced K/Th/U/ratios and magnetic/gravity context. Medium cost; band-6 identity remains provisional, and lithology/soil can mimic the signal.
  4. LiDAR scarp asymmetry cross-checked against deep structure. High cost; only if free official sources, licenses, acquisition coverage and independent mapping checks are verified. Roads and stream banks are major alternatives.
  5. Thermal wells/springs as Phase-2 context only. Never treat resource occurrence as a fault-positive label or a substitute for fault validation; wells are spatially biased.

No follow-up uses the failed holdout to tune thresholds. A new preregistration and fresh buffered spatial test are required; any external raster must first have source/license/coverage provenance checked.
