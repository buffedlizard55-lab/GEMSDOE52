What the official sources say (checked 2026-10-10)

  • Metric. Distance-weighted Tversky index with α = 0.2, β = 0.8, triangular kernel R = 300 m; predictions must lie in [0, 1]. Source: DrivenData problem page (page/967), Performance metric section.
  • Format. Single-layer float32, EPSG 32611, 100 m, same bounds as the training data, "data outside the bounds is null or nan". Source: same page, Submission format section.
  • Public board, live fetch 2026-10-10. #1 xiaofanhu 0.3774 · #2 JerryDataWorks 0.3418 · #8 DARD 0.3195 · #22 extradr19 0.2778. These are team-level public numbers, not filename-linked. Source: leaderboard.
  • Euler deconvolution. Reid, A. B., Allsop, J. M., Granser, H., Millett, A. J., & Somerton, I. W. (1990). Magnetic interpretation in three dimensions using Euler deconvolution. Geophysics 55(1), 80–91. doi:10.1190/1.1442774.
  • Structural index. Table 1 of Reid & Thurston (2014) gives magnetic SI 0 for an infinite contact or fault, 1 for a thin sheet edge, 2 for a pipe, 3 for a sphere. Source: Reid & Thurston 2014 PDF. The H86-E arm uses SI 0 for the contact geometry of a fault trace.

The 0.2778 question, answered

Verified this round from the restored files: the 0.2778 raster (h33-2-b2, 37,654 cells) is a strict subset of the owner-reported 0.2600 raster (d2_8, 44,090 cells). The 6,436 cells it deleted all lie 100–200 m from a mapped trace, so they earn no credit under the metric's kernel but still cost false-positive mass. Deleting them raises the ratio of credited to emitted mass. The score rose 0.2600 → 0.2778 by removing zero-credit cells, not by a better detector. The full metric algebra is in knowledge/49.
