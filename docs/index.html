What makes this unique

Unlike all prior submissions in the GEMSDOE family, H59 uses a multi-scale edge coherence approach:

  1. View A (Geophysical): Gradient magnitude of gravity anomaly, RTP magnetic, and geodetic strain at 3 Gaussian scales (σ=1,2,3). Captures subsurface contacts at multiple depths.
  2. View B (Surface): Radiometric total count edges, DEM slope, and conductivity. Captures surface expression of structure.
  3. Co-training disagreement: Where View A is confident (high edge response) but View B abstains (low surface signal) → buried fault beneath cover. 532,408 A-only candidate pixels identified.
  4. Fault-tip continuation: Exponential proximity boost (2km decay) near catalogue fault tips — the most likely location for uncatalogued extensions.
  5. Metric-aware placement: Greedy emission with the triangular kernel k(d) = max(1−d/300m, 0) ensures each pixel is placed to maximize coverage of potential new-fault pixels.

Why this should score higher

The analysis in GEMSDOE32 established that at DTI 0.28, the acceptance rule is: emit a pixel iff it is within 224m of an uncatalogued fault pixel. The only lever that moves the score is the ranking of candidates — specifically "how likely is there an uncatalogued fault pixel within 2px of here?"

H59's ranking combines three independent signals that have not been used together before:

  • Multi-scale geophysical edges: Different Gaussian scales capture contacts at different depths, increasing the chance of detecting buried fault segments invisible at any single scale.
  • Co-training disagreement: The A-only signal (geophysical edge without surface expression) specifically targets buried faults beneath alluvial cover — the exact class of faults missing from the USGS/INGENIOUS catalogue.
  • Fault-tip proximity: Geological principle: faults propagate laterally; the most likely location for an uncatalogued extension is near the tip of an existing mapped segment.
