  • Exact-pattern uniqueness: PASS within checked scope. Canonical decoded raster hash differs from all 567 unique byte contents among 772 aligned paths (205 byte-identical path aliases collapsed).   • Support novelty: FAIL (0%). Every selected cell is touched by at least one prior’s proposal support; the prior union covers 5,364,328 cells. This is a separate strict ≥20% diagnostic, and it fails because the comparison union is saturated by broad/dense exploratory maps. It is disclosed, not re-labelled as a pass.
  • Not merely the views’ union: PASS. The candidate differs from the same-budget max-union in 35,236 of its 37,654 cells and from the literal union of View A/B. This is a structural comparison, not evidence of geological correctness.
  • Promotion: FAIL. The spatial performance gate fails; the support-novelty diagnostic also fails; no independent confirmation or organizer-format acceptance was obtained. Upload is unauthorized.

What the TIFF represents

The raster is a new inference from SHA-pinned owner-maintained mirrors of the competition rasters; those input bytes were not authenticated by DrivenData. The public sibling TIFF corpus was consulted only after prediction generation, for comparison—not as a model feature or training label.

View A uses potential-field/subsurface predictors. View B includes topographic predictors and band 6, assigned as radiometric-total-count-like only as a measured working hypothesis: its provenance tag conflicts with the spectral comparison, and the official survey page does not establish the exact competition-band mapping.

The A-only reasoning CSV describes all 1,137 connected components (36,540 candidate pixels), with measured band context and alternatives. Of 37,654 emitted cells, 3,030 came from A-only components; none are verified faults. B-only candidates are retained as possible surface artifacts, not asserted to be artifacts.

Why the owner-reported H33 score was 0.2778—and what we can actually establish

The official task is fault mapping. The leaderboard exposes team-level scores, not upload filenames or raster hashes; the H33 filename-to-0.2778 association is therefore owner-reported, not independently authenticated from the public board. The 7 October snapshot lists 0.2778 at rank 13, 0.3195 at rank 7, and 0.3774 at rank 1—not 0.3195 as the overall high.

We fetched the named H33 raster and checked its bytes: 37,654 binary cells, zero on the supplied mapped-catalogue pixels, and a nearest mapped-catalogue distance of about 223.6 m. A sibling H27-4 artifact has 40,199 cells; H33 removes exactly 2,545 off-catalogue cells within 200 m, adds none, and leaves no H33 cells within that ring. The owner-reported paired score is 0.2708→0.2778 (+2.5849%). This is consistent with reducing weak incremental-cover/false-positive mass, but hidden truth and an authenticated upload receipt are unavailable, so it is not causal proof.

The metric weights fault pixels within a 300 m triangular kernel and penalizes unsupported prediction mass (α=0.2, β=0.8). At this sparse binary budget, placement and spatial coverage matter; removing mass can help if it mostly removes false-positive tax, but that is a testable explanation, not proof of why H33 scored as it did. See the byte-level H33 autopsy and official metric description.

Research priority

The preregistered hypothesis ranking remains in H57’s hypothesis record. H57-H1, asymmetric corrected-view disagreement, was tested first and lost. Next candidates are: (1) conductivity termination at an independent potential-field intersection; (2) radiometric flank contrast aligned with a structural step; (3) LiDAR scarp asymmetry cross-checked against deep structure; and (4) thermal-well/spring context for Phase 2 only, never as fault labels. Their costs, failure modes, trusted source links, and preregistered scope are in the record. Do not change hypotheses or spend a slot without a new preregistration and spatially blocked validation.
