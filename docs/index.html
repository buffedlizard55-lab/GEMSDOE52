Why the 0.2778 file scored what it did, and what would actually beat it.

The metric is a distance-weighted Tversky index. With FNw = |G| − TPw it collapses to DTI = T / (0.2·T + 0.2·(S − M) + 0.8·|G|), where T is credited mass, S is emitted mass and M is emitted mass that sits within 300 m of a truth pixel. Every emitted pixel that is not near a fault costs 0.2 in the denominator. That single identity, measured against the restored bytes of the 0.2778 file, is the whole story.

Why the champion 0.2778 file won, and what beats it

Full derivation in knowledge/01 and knowledge/10. Short version: h33-h33-2-b2 = the 0.2600 dotted-ridge file minus the ≤200 m catalogue ring — free precision, because masked pixels can never earn credit but always pay the false-positive tax. The metric's algebra then makes the champion file's double-corroborated core (h33-2-b2 ∩ gems24-d1-5, 25,517 px) credit exactly bracketable at [4,168, 5,223] truth-pixel-mass — 16.3–20.5 % density against 2.79 % for uniform random. That core alone projects 0.2546–0.3190 before any new geology; beating 0.2778 is therefore an arm-ranking problem, and the entire H59 slate attacks exactly that.
