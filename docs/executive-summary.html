1. Core Problem & Strategic Thesis (Maximize P(Win) & Own the Outcome)

The DOE GEMS Prize evaluates fault predictions across a 3730 × 3292 grid at 100 m resolution in EPSG:32611 (5,167,373 active footprint pixels in the Great Basin) using the Distance-Weighted Tversky Index (DTI) with α = 0.2 (false-positive weight), β = 0.8 (false-negative weight), and a 300 m (3 px) linear triangular distance kernel k(d) = max(0, 1 - d/3) (DrivenData Page 967).

Because the known USGS/INGENIOUS fault catalogue (60,988 pixels in labels.tif) is excluded during live scoring, the evaluation target G consists entirely of unmapped off-catalogue faults. Single-learner models trained naively on labels.tif suffer from severe sampling bias: mapped faults are disproportionately located along exposed topographic range fronts, whereas hidden geothermal reservoirs are frequently controlled by buried intra-basin faults beneath Cenozoic alluvial/playa cover.

To overcome this sampling bias without amplifying single-view noise, we implement Blum & Mitchell (COLT ’98, DOI:10.1145/279943.279962) Two-View Co-Training between two physically independent sensor modalities:

  • View A (Potential-Field & Subsurface — 17 bands in training_features.tif): Gravity, magnetics (including Band 6 tc tilt derivative), geodetic strain, seismicity, basement depth, and subsurface electrical conductivity.
  • View B (Surface — 2 DEM bands in training_features.tif + USGS GeoDAWN / 3DEP): Detrended DEM elevation/slope, multi-scale curvature/scarp ridges, 3DEP LiDAR scarps, and airborne gamma-ray radiometrics (K, Th, U, TC).
