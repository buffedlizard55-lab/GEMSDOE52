Method: two views, their disagreement, and the metric's own acceptance rule

Source of truth: src/gems52/. This page explains what the code does and what it measured, including the part that failed.

1 The metric, reduced to what it decides

The official definition (verbatim on page 967):

DTI = TPw / (alpha·FPw + beta·FNw + eps)
alpha = 0.2   beta = 0.8   R = 300 m = 3 px at 100 m
k(d) = max(1 − d/R, 0)
TPw  = Σ_{g∈G} max_x  p(x)·k(x,g)         FPw = Σ_{x: p(x)>0} p(x)·(1 − max_{g∈G} k(x,g))

Three consequences, each pinned by a test in tests/test_metric.py, drive every design decision below:

  • FNw ≡ |G| − TPw. Missing and hitting share one currency, so the denominator is 0.2·(T + S − M) + 0.8·|G| with S the emitted mass — i.e. every pixel you emit costs 0.2 unless it is within 300 m of a truth pixel, and it costs 0.2·(1−k) partially even when it is near one.
  • The optimal mass on an accepted pixel is exactly 1.0. For a single pixel against a single truth pixel, DTI = λk / (0.2λ + 0.8), strictly increasing in λ. So a continuous, "calibrated" probability surface is provably worse than committing. We emit {0,1}.
  • The marginal acceptance rule. Adding a pixel is worth it iff gain(x) > [α·DTI/(1−α·DTI)]·(1 − wmax(x)) where gain is the extra covered mass and wmax the best weight already achieved there. On a 100 m grid the kernel only ever takes these values: 1.000, 0.667, 0.529, 0.333, 0.255, 0.057, 0.0 at d = 0, 1, √2, 2, √5, 2√2, 3 px.

2 The two views

View A — geophysical (19 layers)

A_mag_edge_vg · A_mag_step · A_mag_strike · A_grav_step · A_grav_edge · A_grav_strike · A_strain_inv_reg · A_strain_inv_rank · A_shear_rank · A_grav_vg_rank · A_grav_hg_rank · A_rtp_rank · A_cond_rank · A_depth_base_rank · A_eqdens_rank · A_eqdist_negrank · A_grav_slope_band · A_mag_tilt_abs · A_dilate_rank

The subsurface: potential-field offsets, their strikes, the 2nd invariant of the geodetic tensor, and the official depth-to-basement surface (band 15), which is the cover-thickness proxy.

View B — surface (8 layers)

B_scarp_p900 · B_scarp_p300 · B_scarp_fine · B_scarp_strike · B_slope_rank · B_elev_grad · B_line_resp · B_elev_rank

What morphometry can see: multi-scale scarp response, slope, the directional response of the elevation field. This is the same evidence family the catalogue was built from — which is exactly why B withholds carries information about what the catalogue missed.

All 27 layers enter as uint8 percentile ranks inside the footprint (cotrain.build_stack_from_dir) — one layer resident at a time, because 27 float32 grids on a 12.28 M-pixel box is 700 MB and this machine has 3 GB. Ranks keep the learners linear-model-friendly and make the field scale-free; the raw bands are re-read only where a physical statement is made (cover depth, gravity and magnetic step).

3 Co-training, and what it did

Per Blum & Mitchell (COLT '98): fit each view on the labelled set, then re-fit each view adding the other view's confident pseudo-labels taken from unlabelled pixels — restricted to pixels where the labelling view is in its top 1−q tail (q = 0.995) and the receiving view withholds (score at or below its 60th percentile). Negatives are footprint pixels ≥ 3 px from any catalogue pixel (a 300 m kernel would otherwise teach the model that the trace is background). Pseudo-labels are never drawn from the catalogue, because catalogue pixels are masked out of the real metric and are therefore worthless as a target.

Result: the round hurt. On the full grid, View A's blocked AUC fell in every fold — 0.731→0.709, 0.750→0.710, 0.804→0.769, 0.824→0.793 (pooled 0.797→0.761, mean ΔAUC −0.016, 1/4 folds improved). evidence/co_train_folds_*.json has the per-fold numbers and registry/preregistration.json has the rule that was written before any fold was scored. The gate said stop, so the submitted field does not contain the pseudo-label round.

Read the two bold rows together. A-only pixels sit in 1.8× deeper cover than the mapped-fault population and still carry a 2.3× larger gravity step at comparable slope — offset without relief, which is the signature of a fault under alluvium. B-only pixels have slope without a field offset, which is the signature of a cut, a levee or an erosion line. Both statements are measured on the official bands inside a 15 px corridor off the visible catalogue (evidence/strata_hide.json), and both were written into the per-candidate reasoning table of the submission.

Caveat we do not hide: A-only's ranking is not good enough to emit on. On both instruments, top-K of the A-only field is at or below uniform-random emission at equal budget (Validation). So H52-1 remains a correct description of the population and is used only as a corroboration and down-weighting term — not as the emitter.

5 Emission

emit.greedy_emit implements the marginal rule exactly: expected truth mass is a calibrated density (rescaled so its total equals the inferred |G| estimate, which is what makes a gain comparable to a bar), gain_field is the 29-shift disc convolution with the wrap band zeroed (never np.roll semantics — a fault 40 m from the east edge is not adjacent to one 40 m from the west edge), and candidates are taken lazily from a top-K pool, accepting a pixel only while its recomputed gain clears bar·(1−wmax). The scan gives up after 1,000 consecutive rejections; that approximation is recorded in the returned stats as rejects_at_stop rather than assumed away.

Two implementation rules that came from earlier failures in this family and are now structural: labelled negatives keep a 3 px collar, and holdout assigns whole 8-connected components to folds by majority block with a 4 px boundary buffer, because a fold that contains half a trace is not a test of discovery.
