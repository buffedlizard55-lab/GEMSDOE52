What can explain the reported score change?

The owner-reported attribution pairs H27-4 at 0.2708 with H33 at 0.2778 (+2.5849% relative). For that pairing to become organizer-authenticated, we would need the official upload receipt or score-to-file hash from the competition account; neither is present in this repository. The public snapshot instead lists the team-level 0.2778 at rank 13.

The edit is a near-catalogue-flank ablation: it removes 2,545 off-catalogue proposals within 200 m while retaining 37,654 more distant dots. This is compatible with a metric benefit from removing mass that has little incremental coverage of the hidden test faults and pays weighted false-positive cost. It is not proof that this was the cause of the reported gain. A strict subset cannot increase TPw or reduce FNw for a fixed hidden truth; it can lower FPw. A paired score increase would require the FP reduction to outweigh the lost max-cover credit, which cannot be separated without the hidden truth and authenticated paired upload scores.

Known mapped pixels are masked under the published scoring clarification; they do not receive credit and do not themselves pay the false-positive penalty. The removed 2,545 points were not on those known pixels—they were neighbouring off-mask points. It is therefore inaccurate to say H33 improved by simply deleting known labels.

Metric: placement and incremental coverage, not a distance slogan

The official distance-weighted Tversky metric uses a 300 m triangular kernel, α=0.2 and β=0.8. At 100 m cell spacing:

k(d)  = max(1 − d/300 m, 0)
TPw   = Σtruth g maxprediction x p(x) · k(d(x,g))
FPw   = Σprediction x p(x) · [1 − maxtruth g k(d(x,g))]
FNw   = |G| − TPw
DTI   = TPw / (TPw + 0.2·FPw + 0.8·FNw)

For a candidate addition with incremental max-cover credit c and false-positive increment f, the exact improvement condition is c(1 − 0.2·DTI) > 0.2·DTI·f. Only in the special case of a single previously uncovered truth pixel does that reduce to a simple distance-only bar. Nearby predictions compete for the same truth pixel’s maximum cover, so no universal “emit within X metres” rule follows from the score alone.

At DTI 0.2778, the one-new-truth-pixel special-case bar is 0.05556. A 200 m diagonal at 100 m grid spacing has kernel weight 0.05719 and clears that special-case bar. This is not a general cutoff: prior coverage and multiple hidden fault pixels change the incremental credit.

Leaderboard correction and interpretation

The dated official-board snapshot for 7 October lists 0.3774 at rank 1, 0.3195 at rank 7, and 0.2778 at rank 13. Thus 0.3195 was not the highest public score in that snapshot. The board reports team-level best scores, not filenames, hashes, hidden labels, or upload receipts; the association between the 0.2778 row and the named H33 raster remains an owner-reported attribution.

At fixed truth and false-positive mass, moving from 0.2778 to 0.3774 would require approximately 1.388× as much weighted TP. That is conditional algebra, not a forecast and not a conclusion that the leader used any particular geology or algorithm. Public submission TIFFs do not reveal the hidden labels.
