2. Detailed Geological Specification of All 5 Candidate Hypotheses

Rank #1 (H52-1): Blum & Mitchell (1998) Two-View Co-Training + Disagreement-Driven Buried Fault Discovery

  • Data Layers Combined:
    • View A (Subsurface / Potential-Field — 17 bands in training_features.tif): mag_anom (B1), rtp (B2), tmi_hg (B3), geod_2ndinv (B4), iso_grav_anom_slope (B5), tc (B6), geod_shearrate (B7), geod_dilaterate (B8), tmi_vg (B9), deq_n100a15 (B10), iso_grav_anom_vg (B11), iso_grav_anom (B13), tmi (B14), depth_to_base_surf (B15), ieq_n100a15 (B16), cond_surf (B17), iso_grav_anom_hg (B18).
    • View B (Surface — 2 bands in training_features.tif + External USGS Rasters): det_elev (B12), det_elev_slope (B19), data/external/geodawn_rad_u8.tif (K, Th, U, TC, DOI:10.5066/P93LGLVQ), data/external/geodawn_extensions_u8.tif (UK), and data/external/lidar_scarp_features_u8.tif.
  • Physical Signature: Partitioning subsurface physics (View A) from surface morphology/geochemistry (View B) enables iterative whole-segment pseudo-labeling where one view is confident (≥98.5th percentile) and the other abstains (25th–78th percentile), followed by explicit disagreement decomposition: Corroborated (A ∧ B) + Buried Discovery (A ∖ B under cover) - Surface Artifact (B ∖ A on flat valley floors).
  • Why It Catches Missing Catalogue Faults: Field geologists mapping the USGS Quaternary fault catalogue rely primarily on visible surface scarps (`View B`), systematically missing intra-basin step-over faults buried beneath Quaternary alluvium/playa sediments (`depth_to_base_surf`) and regions lacking 3DEP LiDAR coverage (`~lvalid`, 25% of footprint). Conversely, pure DEM detectors trigger false positives on stream terraces, shoreline bars, and roads where `View A` shows zero basement offset.
  • How It Differs from Prior Implementations: Prior repos (`GEMSDOE19`–`GEMSDOE46`) either used early-fusion single learners, static hand-weighted linear averages, or post-hoc pruning of the frozen `H19_5` mask. None implemented Blum & Mitchell (1998) two-view co-training, empirical OOF negative-error conditional independence testing (`r = +0.10143`), or B-only surface artifact suppression paired with A-only buried fault discovery.

Rank #2 (H52-2): Miller & Singh (1994) Magnetic Tilt-Angle Zero-Crossing Ridge (tc = 0 ∩ |∇tc|)

  • Data Layers Combined: Official `training_features.tif` Band 6 (`tc`, magnetic tilt angle), Band 2 (`rtp`, reduced-to-pole magnetic anomaly), Band 3 (`tmi_hg`, horizontal gradient), Band 9 (`tmi_vg`, vertical gradient), and Band 15 (`depth_to_base_surf`).
  • Physical Signature: Under Miller & Singh (1994) potential-field theory, the magnetic tilt angle $\theta = \arctan(\partial M / \partial z \,/\, \|\nabla_h M\|)$ equals $0^\circ$ directly over vertical magnetic susceptibility contacts regardless of source depth, while the horizontal gradient of the tilt angle $\|\nabla \theta\|$ peaks along the contact strike.
  • Why It Catches Missing Catalogue Faults: Standard magnetic horizontal gradient (`tmi_hg`) decays as $z^{-3}$ with burial depth $z$, losing sensitivity over deep intra-basin faults. Because `tc` is a ratio of vertical to horizontal derivatives, its zero-crossing is depth-invariant and traces deeply buried volcanic/plutonic fault offsets beneath basin fill.
  • How It Differs from Prior Implementations: Prior attempts either treated `tc` as an ordinary raw value in tree models or misclassified `tc` as radiometric Total Count (`IR-52-01`). We explicitly extract the zero-crossing ridge $\exp(-|\text{tc} - \text{med}(\text{tc})| / \sigma_{\text{tc}}) \cdot \|\nabla \text{tc}\|$ modulated by structure-tensor coherence.

Rank #3 (H52-3): 3D Isostatic Gravity Analytic Signal + Basement Depth Step (depth_to_base_surf)

  • Data Layers Combined: Official `training_features.tif` Band 13 (`iso_grav_anom`), Band 18 (`iso_grav_anom_hg`), Band 11 (`iso_grav_anom_vg`), Band 5 (`iso_grav_anom_slope`), and Band 15 (`depth_to_base_surf`).
  • Physical Signature: High 3D gravity analytic signal amplitude $\|\mathbf{A}_g\| = \sqrt{(\text{grav\_hg})^2 + (\text{grav\_vg})^2}$ co-located with steep horizontal gradients in basement depth $\|\nabla(\text{depth\_to\_base\_surf})\|$ and second-order Hessian ridges of `iso_grav_anom`.
  • Why It Catches Missing Catalogue Faults: Basin-and-Range horst/graben blocks juxtapose dense Paleozoic/Mesozoic basement ($\rho \approx 2.67\text{ g/cm}^3$) against low-density Cenozoic basin fill ($\rho \approx 2.1\text{ g/cm}^3$). Concealed accommodation faults within grabens produce sharp density steps in `iso_grav_anom` and `depth_to_base_surf` even when Holocene alluvial fans completely mask the surface scarp.
  • How It Differs from Prior Implementations: Prior implementations used raw `iso_grav_anom` or `iso_grav_anom_hg` alone (which produces broad 1–2 km halos that fail the 300 m DTI kernel). We sharpen the 3D analytic signal with structure-tensor coherence and localize it along subtle surface/radiometric contacts.

Rank #4 (H52-4): Subsurface Electrical Conductivity Boundary (cond_surf) + GeoDAWN K/Th Hydrothermal Alteration

  • Data Layers Combined: Official `training_features.tif` Band 17 (`cond_surf`), Band 4 (`geod_2ndinv`), Band 7 (`geod_shearrate`), plus external USGS GeoDAWN airborne gamma-ray spectrometry `data/external/geodawn_rad_u8.tif` (`K`, `Th`, `U`, DOI:10.5066/P93LGLVQ) and `data/external/geodawn_extensions_u8.tif` (`UK`).
  • Physical Signature: High lateral gradients in subsurface electrical conductivity $\|\nabla(\text{cond\_surf})\|$ paired with elevated potassium-to-thorium (`K/Th`) and uranium-to-potassium (`U/K`) radiometric gradients along linear structure-tensor trends.
  • Why It Catches Missing Catalogue Faults: Active geothermal upflow zones circulate saline fluids and alter host rock into conductive smectite/illite clays (`cond_surf`) while precipitating potassium-bearing adularia/illite at the surface (`K/Th` ratio anomaly), even along blind faults with minimal vertical topographic throw.
  • How It Differs from Prior Implementations: `GEMSDOE46` used `K` and `Th/K` gradients without coupling them to subsurface electrical conductivity (`cond_surf`, Band 17) or two-view co-training.

Rank #5 (H52-5): External Sentinel-1 InSAR Phase-Gradient Discontinuity + Microseismicity Alignment

  • Data Layers Combined: External Sentinel-1 ascending/descending LOS velocity interferograms + official `training_features.tif` Band 10 (`deq_n100a15`) and Band 16 (`ieq_n100a15`).
  • Physical Signature: Short-wavelength phase-gradient discontinuities across creeping or interseismically locked faults.
  • Why Deferred: High external ingestion cost and atmospheric/ionospheric noise over Great Basin playas; Bands 4, 7, 8 (`geod_2ndinv`, `geod_shearrate`, `geod_dilaterate`) and Bands 10, 16 (`deq_n100a15`, `ieq_n100a15`) are already integrated inside View A at zero additional cost.
