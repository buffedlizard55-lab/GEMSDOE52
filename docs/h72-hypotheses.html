Rank 1 · H72-A · tested

Strain-only geophysical teacher

LayersView A exactly `raw_band_04` (second invariant of the strain-rate tensor), `raw_band_07` (geodetic shear rate), and signed `raw_band_08` (dilatation rate). View B stays fixed on the shared DEM-derived surface and available radiometric channels.
Physical signatureSpatially coherent shear/strain anomaly with a dilatational contrast, ranked only where OOF View A is confident and fixed View B abstains.
Why an undiscovered fault?A concealed or basin-covered structure could localize deformation while relief and surface radiometry remain ambiguous. Catalogue incompleteness makes that possible; it does not make the signal fault-specific.
What differs from repo methods?Earlier View-A learners combined strain with gravity, magnetics, seismicity, or other subsurface channels. H72 isolates these three existing deformation channels as the entire View A; it adds no external layer, new transform, or replacement method.
Expected DTI improvementHighest relative priority among the viable existing-data candidates, but likely modest and uncertain. No numeric improvement was forecast. The actual candidate HOLDOUT-DTI was underfilled in fold 2; no matched candidate-minus-single-B result is valid.
Cost and named mimicModerate: refit existing shared learners and run the registered tests. Mimic: broad interseismic loading, a geodetic survey seam, or an interpolation edge rather than a discrete fault.
Observed outcomeNegative: pre-placement surface test passed, final-dot literal and policy tests stopped, support novelty was zero, and no H72 TIFF was written. See the full result.
Rank 2 · H72-B · not tested

Magnetic-edge-only geophysical teacher

LayersRTP magnetic anomaly `raw_band_02`; TMI horizontal/vertical derivatives `raw_band_03` and `raw_band_09`; TMI `raw_band_14`; and existing 150 m upward-continued TMI channels (`X_mag_TMI_up150_*`). View B remains unchanged.
Physical signatureA coherent magnetic-source boundary that persists between shallow and upward-continued products, at locations where View B abstains.
Why an undiscovered fault?A buried basement discontinuity can generate a magnetic edge under basin cover without a clear scarp or surface lineament; it could reveal a continuation absent from the visible fault catalogue.
What differs from repo methods?Magnetic channels appeared in broad mixed View A models, but a magnetic-family-only teacher has not been isolated as one co-training view. The composition changes; the lane protocol does not.
Expected DTI improvementLow. Magnetic edges are structurally plausible but can be broad and non-fault-specific. No numeric projection.
Cost and named mimicModerate: use existing cached/restored layers and the same learner/evaluator. Mimics: lithologic contacts, flight-line or tie-line artifacts, and magnetic sources unrelated to fault displacement.
Rank 3 · H72-C · narrow composition / novelty not verified

Seismo-kinematic geophysical teacher

LayersEarthquake distance and density `raw_band_10` and `raw_band_16`, combined with geodetic shear and dilatation `raw_band_07` and `raw_band_08`. Keep fixed DEM/radiometric View B unchanged.
Physical signatureA coherent event-density plus strain/shear corridor where surface View B abstains.
Why an undiscovered fault?Active seismicity and deformation may mark an unmapped structural corridor or a covered continuation that has little surface relief.
What differs from repo methods?H74 A2 already used all four proposed raw inputs (`raw_band_07`, `_08`, `_10`, `_16`) within its broader deformation/seismicity view and transforms. H72-C is a narrower four-channel teacher, but its novelty/distinctness versus H74 and prior mixed methods has not been established.
Expected DTI improvementVery low / uncertain. Seismic density may be sparse or catalog-dependent; no numeric projection.
Cost and named mimicLow to moderate: existing competition channels, shared pipeline. Mimics: earthquake swarm, volcanic or hydrothermal unrest, or an event-catalogue density artifact.
Rank 4 · H72-D · data-blocked, not tested

Heat-flow-gradient teacher

LayersA gradient/edge transform of the official Great Basin heat-flow grid would be added to geophysical View A; View B would remain fixed. This layer is not in the H72 cache or competition feature stack.
Physical signatureA broad thermal-gradient transition aligned with an A-confident/B-abstaining region; heat flow is regional and not inherently fault-specific.
Why an undiscovered fault?A buried permeable structure could influence heat transport without a surface expression; the physical pathway is possible, but non-unique.
What differs from repo methods?It would introduce an external data layer that is absent from the registered H72 views. It cannot be smuggled into or substituted for the frozen H72-A test.
Expected DTI improvementPotentially interesting but unknown; not currently viable. No data payload was obtained or validated, so no expected score is asserted.
Cost and named mimicHigh and data-blocked: locate official bytes, confirm rights and resolution, hash them, align them, and preregister a separate test. Mimics: lithologic variation, groundwater advection, or sparse-well interpolation.
