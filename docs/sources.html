§1Rules, metric and format — the load-bearing four

claim we rely onsourcewhere to look
The metric is distance-weighted Tversky: DTI = TPw / (TPw + α·FPw + β·FNw + ε) with α = 0.2, β = 0.8, R = 300 m = 3 px, k(d) = max(1 − d/R, 0), TPw = Σg∈G max_x p(x)·k, FPw = Σx: p>0 p(x)·[1 − max_g k]. Their worked example: 3.00 / 1.89 / 2.00 → 0.60. Competition rules / evaluation page (page 967) “Evaluation” section, verbatim. Re-read 2026-10-06 21:33 UTC. Our src/gems52/metric.py implements this text and is pinned by 8 unit tests, including the worked example.
Submission format: single band, float32, values in [0, 1], EPSG:32611, 100 m cells, identical bounds to sample_submission.tif, null or NaN outside the valid footprint. same page, “Submission format” Encoded in src/gems52/grid.py (pinned shape/transform) and enforced by src/gems52/gates.py. The null/NaN sentence contradicts the validator — see IR-52-006.
The scoring mask is pixel-exact and identical to the training fault labels; only new-fault truth counts; a prediction near a known trace but far from new truth is fully penalised; and new-fault truth can lie within 300 m of a known trace — finding those corrections is a goal of the competition. Community: “Discussion on evaluation masking and new fault truth”, post #4 chrisk-dd (staff), 21 Sep. This one post is why the corridor regime exists at all, and why “predict the catalogue” is worth nothing.
No details of the test faults are given; the Phase 2 pool is built from a test set updated by expert review of all Phase 1 submissions. Community: “Discussion on the public leaderboard and final ranking”, #7 chrisk-dd (staff), 23 Sep. Consequence: the public score is a signal, not a bound. Mitigation on this site is the per-segment reasoning table published beside the raster.

§3Ground-truth provenance of the provided grids

  • NLR / DOE-GEMS rules document (www.nlr.gov/docs/fy26osti/96647.pdf) — the canonical programme document the brief cites as docs.nlr.gov; that host does not serve it, see IR-52-004.
  • Organiser's reference solution — UNet + masked cross-entropy with a Tversky loss and no scoring code, which is why our metric.py was written from the rules text and then unit-tested against their worked example instead of being ported.
  • A competitor's DTI reimplementation with a held-out-segment protocol and a 3-px buffer — used here only as a parity reference for our own implementation.
