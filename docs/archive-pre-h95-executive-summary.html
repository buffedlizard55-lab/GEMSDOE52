  1. Download h95-candidate.tif (132,513 bytes) or the ZIP. The organiser's form accepts either a single-band GeoTIFF or a ZIP containing exactly one; the ZIP here contains exactly this TIFF and nothing else, re-verified at publish time.
  2. Check the file you actually downloaded on the browser checker. It decodes every pixel locally and reports each published rule as PASS or FAIL. Confirm the SHA-256 is c122a9ff37abf5512bbddf39c9d55c60effb2229ed992d959e357ffb804a50f3. If it is not, you downloaded something else — a truncated transfer, or a GitHub Pages 404 page saved with a .tif extension, which is the single most common cause of a confusing rejection.
  3. Sign in at the competition page and open Submit.
  4. Upload the file. The form rejects anything whose predicted values fall outside [0, 1] with “Predicted values must be in range [0, 1]”. This file's values are exactly {0, 1}, with 0 non-finite pixels anywhere in the grid, so it cannot trip that rule.

2Unnormalised model scores (log-odds, distances, z-scores) the checker reports min/max far outside [0,1]normalise to [0,1], or emit binary {0,1}

4A NoData sentinel outside [0,1] (e.g. −3.4e38) inside the grid a huge negative minimum plus a GDAL_NODATA tag write NaN or 0 outside the footprint, never the float32 sentinel

What this session measured about cause 5. Of the 13 owner-scored rasters restored into data/scored/ and data/reference/, 11 are stripped/LZW with nodata=nan and 7,111,787 NaN outside the footprint, and 2 are all-finite with zeros outside and no nodata tag. One of those two is h33-2-b2-zeros.tif, the OWNER-REPORTED 0.2778 file. Both containers have therefore been accepted by the portal, so the container alone cannot be the whole explanation for a range rejection — but the all-finite container cannot trip the rule under any reader, NaN-honouring or not.
