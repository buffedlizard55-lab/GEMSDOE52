Mechanism, mimic, falsifier

Hypothesis. Thermal-gap hypothesis: a hot spring / high-geothermometry well that the VISIBLE fault catalogue cannot explain (distance to the nearest visible catalogue fault > 3 px = 300 m) is surface evidence of a fault that is absent from that catalogue. Co-training gates the emission: the candidate is accepted where View A (potential-field / subsurface) is confident about structure and View B (surface) abstains, which is the standing brief's A-only 'buried fault' population; the reverse disagreement (B confident, A abstains) is vetoed as a surface artefact.

Mechanism. Hot springs and high-geothermometry wells discharge along fault-controlled fluid pathways. Where the VISIBLE catalogue has no fault within 300 m of such a site, the feeding structure is a candidate the catalogue lacks; the two-view co-training field decides whether it is accepted (View A confident, View B abstaining = buried) or vetoed (View B confident, View A abstaining = surface artefact).

Named non-fault mimic. fracture mesh without a through-going fault; shallow aquifer / irrigation return flow; lithologic contact or intrusive margin; basin-margin facies step; paleo-channel; road cut and erosion lines (the vetoed B-only set)

Disagreement (the brief's signal). A-confident / B-abstains is the buried population; B-confident / A-abstains is vetoed as a surface artefact. The measured A-only share of the emitted dots is 0.3870.

Limits and remaining work

  • The holdout instrument measures withheld catalogue faults, not the organisers' newly discovered faults; the off-catalogue instrument (SGMC) is a different, also imperfect, population, and its mass curve is flat, so neither instrument certifies a board gain.
