## 1. Decision before fitting

Those are diagnostic AUCs, **not DTI scores**. H77cond also found that transferring mass from the better single-B control to A reduced HOLDOUT-DTI. The conditional test rejects the specific claim that A is most useful where B is blind; it does not prove that no buried faults exist anywhere. ## 3. What can and cannot be said about the reported H33 result

### Byte-level pattern recorded in the repository

The owner-attributed H33 raster is represented by a hash-pinned owner mirror, `data/reference/h33-2-b2-zeros.tif`; its bytes are not present in this checkout now. The prior byte audit in `knowledge/49` and the later correction in `knowledge/42b` report the following, conditional on those manifest-pinned mirror bytes:

- H33 contains 37,654 positive cells and is a strict subset of the 44,090-cell `gems24-d2-8` raster; no H33 cells are added, and 6,436 cells are removed.
- The removed cells are 100–200 m from the mapped catalogue in that audit. H33 has no cells within 200 m of that catalogue and the measured minimum retained distance is 223.6 m.
- The owner-reported score pair is 0.2600 for the parent and 0.2778 for H33. If that file-to-score association is correct, it is a +0.0178 absolute / about +6.8% relative change while removing about 14.6% of the parent mass. Those are **OWNER-REPORTED file/score attributions**, not organizer-confirmed receipt values.

The measurable pattern is consistent with **selective thinning**: some marginal cells were removed, and the retained support may have carried a higher average contribution on that particular scored split. It does not show that H33 discovered a new detector or that the 100–200 m zone is a geological no-fault zone.

### Correction to older wording

Do **not** repeat the claim that the 6,436 removed cells earned exactly zero credit, that the competition masks a 200 m known-fault buffer, or that the score change is mathematically proven “free precision.” `knowledge/42b_h65halo_results_and_limits.md` and `IR-H65halo-007` correct that earlier overclaim: the official scoring target is the private new-fault set, and the organizer's staff clarification says new truth may lie within 300 m of a known trace. Credit for the deleted cells therefore depends on private truth we do not have. The 300 m distance-weighted Tversky metric can reward nearby truth; whether a deleted cell was a false positive, a redundant prediction, or useful coverage cannot be reconstructed from the catalogue alone.

The official formula uses α=0.2, β=0.8 and a 300 m triangular kernel. That weights false negatives more heavily than false positives, but does **not** mean false positives are free or make deletion automatically beneficial. The score increase is compatible with removal of weak marginal coverage; causality is not identified without the actual file receipt and private truth.

### Scientific interpretation

The strongest defensible interpretation is that this file family benefited from mass selection against a particular evaluation split, not that the deleted ring proves an unmapped-fault mechanism. H33's owner-reported public result is below the current public leader and the current DARD row. No available evidence supports the stronger statement “H33 is scientifically the best geothermal-fault model” or a guaranteed route to beat the live public score. The competition task is **fault mapping indicative of geothermal resources**—not direct confirmation or prediction of geothermal vents.
